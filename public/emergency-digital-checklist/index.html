The emergency digital checklist

Most of us carry our lives in accounts, devices and files that only we can open. That's great for privacy, but hard on the people around us if something unexpected happens. This checklist helps you get organized in an afternoon, then keep it current with a short review once a year.

None of it requires Loomkeeper. Where Loomkeeper can help, we say so, and we also say what it doesn't do.

1. Make a list of what you have

  • Email accounts, especially the one your other accounts use for password resets.
  • Banking, pensions, investments, insurance and any crypto wallets.
  • Utilities, phone plan, subscriptions and anything on automatic payment.
  • Social media, photo libraries and cloud storage.
  • Domains, websites or online businesses you run.
  • Work accounts. Note who at your employer should be told, but don't record work credentials in personal tools.

You don't need passwords on this list. You need to know what exists and where it lives.

2. Secure your passwords and sign-in methods

  • Use a password manager, and learn how its recovery or emergency options work.
  • Save backup codes for two-step verification somewhere safe and offline.
  • Write down how to unlock your phone, or tell someone you trust where that information is kept. Your phone is often the key to everything else.
  • Check the recovery email and phone number on your main accounts. Are they still current?

3. Use the legacy tools your providers already offer

Several big platforms have their own settings for what happens to your account after you die or stop using it. For example, Apple lets you add a Legacy Contact (https://support.apple.com/en-us/102631), and Google's Inactive Account Manager can notify people or share data after your account has been inactive for a period you choose (https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/3036546). Set these up for the accounts that matter most. They only cover each provider's own data.

4. Gather your important documents

  • Identity documents, insurance policies, property and vehicle papers.
  • Your will and any powers of attorney. For anything with legal weight, talk to a licensed attorney where you live.
  • A one-page "who to call" sheet: your doctor, lawyer, accountant, employer and a close friend or relative.

Keep originals where they belong (a safe place at home, or with your lawyer), and make sure at least one person you trust knows where they are.

5. Decide who should know what

  • Choose one or two people who would step in first, and tell them where your list and documents are.
  • Decide who should receive personal things: photos, letters, recordings, family history.
  • Think about what you'd want said, and to whom, if you weren't there to say it.

6. Plan for after your passing, separately from emergencies

An emergency while you're alive and what happens after your passing need different tools.

  • While you're alive (for example during an illness or a hospital stay), the right tools are things like a power of attorney and the emergency access settings in your password manager or bank.
  • After your passing, you'll want your files and personal messages to reach the right people, and nobody else.

That second part is what Loomkeeper is for. You can keep important files in an encrypted Vault and choose which people receive each folder. You can record Afterlife Messages for delivery on a date you choose. And you decide how your passing is confirmed: through Verification Contacts, who vote on whether you've passed away, or through a Trustee you've chosen. With Verification Contacts, we also try to reach you for 60 days before anything is released. Loomkeeper is designed to release your content only after your passing has been confirmed.

7. Review it once a year

Pick a date you'll remember, like your birthday or New Year's, and spend 20 minutes on it. Update your list, check your recovery details, and make sure the people you chose are still the right ones.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a password manager and Loomkeeper?

They do different jobs. A password manager helps you, and anyone you give emergency access, sign in to accounts. Loomkeeper holds files and messages you want delivered to specific people after your passing.

How often should I update my checklist?

At least once a year, and whenever something big changes: a new phone, a new bank, a move or a new family member.
